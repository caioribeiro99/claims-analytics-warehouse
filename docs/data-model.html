Northstar Claims Analytics — Data model & lineage

Referral partners send price lookups, some lookups become claims submitted by providers, and some claims are reversed later. This page shows how those events become three fact tables in a single DuckDB file: validated on arrival, quarantined instead of repaired, costed as of the moment each claim was submitted, and published only when every build invariant holds.

All data is synthetic. Northstar, its partners, providers, products and prices are fictional and generated deterministically by claims_warehouse/synthetic.py from a fixed seed.

Relationships around fct_claims

Three fact tables at three grains, joined through claim_id. Only the edges that the build actually joins on are solid; the dotted edge is available for analysis but never used to filter or price.

As-of pricing

Reference unit costs change over time and arrive as weekly publications that restate the whole price list. A claim is costed with the price that was in effect when it was submitted: the latest effective_date at or before submitted_at.

Equality join on product_code

JOIN ref_product_costs
  USING (product_code)

Returns one row per change-point, three for this claim. Every sum over the result silently multiplies.

Fans out

Newest price overall

max(effective_date)
  per product_code

One row, but 60.16635 took effect on 2025-05-16, weeks after this claim. Past claims are costed with future prices.

Leaks the future

As-of join, used by fct_claims

ASOF LEFT JOIN ref_product_costs AS cost
  ON cost.product_code = c.product_code
 AND c.submitted_at >= cost.effective_date

One row per claim at most: the change-point already in effect, 63.33300 from 2025-02-04.

Correct as of submission

Restatements

The same (product_code, effective_date) appears in many publications, and a later publication can correct a price already published. ref_product_costs keeps one row per change-point with arg_max(unit_cost, published_date), so the latest publication wins, and records first_published_date, last_published_date, publication_rows and was_restated (count(DISTINCT unit_cost) > 1). Every published row stays in staging.cost_publications with its source_file.

Semantics

The facts keep unknowns visible. Most rules below exist so that a sum cannot quietly turn missing information into a number.

Reversal-aware net_* fields

A claim is reversed when at least one contract-valid reversal references it; several reversals still reverse it once, and the earliest dates it. A reversed claim contributes a known zero, even where the underlying value was unknown. Otherwise the submitted value passes through, NULL included.

CASE WHEN is_reversed THEN 0 ELSE partner_payout END AS net_partner_payout

The same rule builds net_gross_amount, net_units, net_service_fee, net_partner_payout, net_retained_fee and net_reference_cost; is_net_claim is 0 or 1. Summing a net_* column equals summing its source column over non-reversed claims, with no filter to forget.

Attribution and partner economics

Claims carry no partner. partner_code, lookup_id and channel come only from the lookup that produced the claim, and the build asserts at most one valid lookup per in-scope claim.

attribution_statusRulepartner_payout
'attributed'has a lookup, partner has terms'flat': flat_payout_cents * 0.01
'revenue_share': service_fee * revenue_share_pct * 0.01
'attributed_unknown_terms'has a lookup, payout_model is NULLNULL
'unattributed'NOT has_lookupNULL

retained_fee = service_fee - partner_payout is the closest observable margin proxy: the part of its fee the platform keeps. It is not accounting margin (the sources hold no operating costs), it is NULL whenever the payout is unknown, and it is negative when a flat payout exceeds a small fee.

The aggregation trap

Within 'unattributed' or 'attributed_unknown_terms' claims, reversed rows hold a known 0 and the rest hold NULL. sum() skips the NULLs and adds the zeros:

0NULLNULL0NULL →sum() = 0.00

That prints as "nothing paid, nothing kept" when the truth is "unknown". Filter to the population where the measure is defined:

SELECT sum(net_partner_payout)
         FILTER (WHERE attribution_status = 'attributed'),
       sum(net_retained_fee)
         FILTER (WHERE attribution_status = 'attributed')
FROM fct_claims;

Three conversion flags, nested

Each flag on fct_lookups implies the one outside it. The gaps between them are diagnostics, not rounding.

has_claim_reference the funnel recorded a conversion: claim_id IS NOT NULL. The business funnel metric.

claim_resolved the id exists on some record in staging.claims_raw, valid or not. Referential health.

in_scope_conversion the claim is in fct_claims. The only basis that may be combined with claim economics; minutes_to_claim is set only here.

resolution_status

Lookups and reversals are never scope-filtered. An unusable claim reference becomes a status and the row stays; the first matching rule wins, top to bottom.

Data quality: validate, never repair

A record that breaks its contract is excluded whole, with every reason it triggered; the fields that did pass are still typed, so a rejected record stays easy to inspect. Nothing is imputed, rounded or salvaged: "ten" is not 10, 19.999 is not 19.99, and 03/14/2025 is not a timestamp. Numbers are checked exactly as delivered: the build parses JSON numbers as Decimal, never as binary floats.

Reconciliation identity

source_records = curated_records + rejected_records

Checked per stream in audit_reconciliation: raw staging rows on the left, the stream's fact plus its rejects rows on the right. Every source record is in exactly one of the two, never both and never neither. Reasons overlap, so per-reason counts in audit_reject_reasons intentionally do not add up to the stream total.

Conditions that are legal but worth watching (unexpected channel labels, lookups with a product_code outside the catalog, reused reversal_id values) are reported by analyses/11_data_quality_observations.sql, never repaired.

Build invariants

Each query in checks.py counts offending rows; any non-zero result aborts the build before the new file replaces the old one. They guard structure. Business semantics are covered by the test suite.

  • Reference contracts
  • dim_provider.provider_id is unique
  • dim_partner.partner_code is unique
  • dim_product.product_code is unique
  • reference keys are never null
  • every partner has exactly one payout term (flat cents or revenue share)
  • partner terms are exact and in range (whole cents >= 0; share 0-100, at most 2 decimals)
  • cost publication rows are complete with exact unit costs (>= 0, at most 5 decimals)
  • a cost publication lists each (product_code, effective_date) at most once
  • ref_product_costs has one row per (product_code, effective_date)
  • Keys & grain
  • no in-scope claim has more than one valid lookup (attribution would fan out)
  • fct_claims.claim_id is unique
  • fct_claims has exactly one row per in-scope claim
  • fct_lookups.lookup_id is unique
  • fct_lookups has exactly one row per contract-valid lookup
  • fct_reversals.reversal_sk is unique
  • fct_reversals has exactly one row per contract-valid reversal
  • Reconciliation
  • every stream reconciles: source = curated + rejected

Why this shape

Separate grains, not one wide table

Lookups, claims and reversals happen at different grains and times. One wide claim table would drop unconverted lookups or repeat a claim per reversal. Each fact keeps a true key; cross-grain questions aggregate each side to a shared grain first, then join.

Quarantine, not repair

A repaired record is a guess that looks like data. rejects keeps every reason, the file and row, and the original payload, so the producer can fix the source, and the reconciliation identity proves nothing vanished. Copies of a claim id that disagree are all quarantined instead of picking a survivor; only the later copies of an exact redelivery, which lose nothing when dropped, are rejected for being repeats.

Effective dating

Prices change and get corrected. The as-of join costs a claim with what was in effect when it was submitted, restatements resolve to the latest publication, and next_effective_date gives the same answer as a range join. The three dimensions are single current snapshots today; they are next in line for the same treatment.

Full reload and an atomic swap

At this scale a rebuild is cheap and deterministic, needs no incremental state, and picks up reversals that arrive weeks late for free. The build writes .claims_warehouse.duckdb.building and renames it into place only when every check passes, so a failed build never destroys a good warehouse and a reader never sees a half-built one.
